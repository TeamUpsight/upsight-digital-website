# Website growth strategy

This is a living strategy, not a fixed delivery plan. The current site already has a clear services hub, audience page, process, contact path, free Health Check and two detailed case studies. It is technically well-positioned for crawlability through prerendered content and shared metadata. The largest opportunity is depth and evidence, not a redesign or a large volume of low-value landing pages.

## Goals

- Improve qualified lead generation.
- Demonstrate technical authority and implementation depth.
- Increase search and AI-system understanding of Upsight Digital.
- Strengthen first-party proof while preserving fast performance.
- Turn case studies and the Health Check into useful acquisition assets.

## High-value opportunities

Create dedicated service pages only where Upsight has approved capability, evidence and a distinct user need; the broad `/services` page currently groups several major topics. Strong candidates to validate are server-side tracking, GA4/GTM implementation, Meta CAPI, tracking audits, ecommerce tracking and dashboards/reporting. Each should explain the problem, implementation scope, limits, proof and a relevant consultation/Health Check action.

Build deeper audience/industry pages only from real experience, such as agency white-label needs or ecommerce measurement complexity. Avoid mechanically combining cities and services or publishing pages solely to cover keywords.

Grow first-party evidence: approved case studies, technical implementation examples and practical guides can make the site more useful than generic service copy. Link case studies to the services demonstrated, and service pages back to relevant evidence. Use the Health Check as an educational conversion asset, with clear follow-up paths rather than unsupported promises.

Improve contextual internal links where they help a visitor choose a next step: service -> case study -> consultation; case study -> service/process -> contact; guide -> service/Health Check. Review proof-point placement and conversion paths as content grows.

Favor fewer substantial pages that answer a real question and show real expertise. Preserve the existing lightweight rendering and asset policy.
